Our Philosophy

Rooted in Tradition. Made for Everyday Cooking.

Across Indian households—especially in South Indian kitchens where morning tiffin and slow-simmered meals anchor the day—heavy cast iron cookware has always held a place of quiet respect. Unlike disposable modern pans, cast iron invites patience, rewards steady flame, and becomes better with every season of use.

The Cast House was conceived around a simple belief: everyday cookware should be honest, uncoated, and enduring. By focusing on pure cast iron silhouettes—from balanced dosa tawas to deep kadais and paniyaram pans—we bring traditional kitchen tools into contemporary homes without unnecessary complexity.

Every piece is pre-seasoned and thoughtfully proportioned for daily cooking, encouraging home cooks to reconnect with tactile, intentional preparation.

First-Time Owner Handbook

Cast Iron Care & Seasoning Guide

Caring for cast iron takes less than two minutes after cooking. Follow these five simple habits to prevent rust and build a smooth natural finish.

01

Clean While Warm

Let the pan cool slightly until safe to handle. Wash with warm water and a soft brush or coir scrubber. Avoid harsh detergents or soaking overnight.

02

Dry Immediately

Wipe completely dry with a clean cotton towel, then place the cookware on low flame for 1–2 minutes until every drop of moisture evaporates.

03

Apply Thin Oil Coat

Rub 3–4 drops of neutral cooking oil (gingelly, groundnut, or vegetable oil) across the inner and outer surface using a paper towel until barely glossy.

04

Store in a Dry Spot

Keep your cookware in a well-ventilated, dry cabinet. If stacking pans, place a cotton cloth or paper liner between them to allow airflow.

05

Basic Re-Seasoning

If food ever sticks, wash, dry, coat lightly with oil, and heat on medium-low flame for 8–10 minutes until lightly smoking. Cool naturally to restore smoothness.
